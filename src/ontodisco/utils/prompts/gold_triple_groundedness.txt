You are checking whether a single, isolated sentence provides enough information on its own to support
a reference (subject, relation, object) fact -- WITHOUT using any outside knowledge, and WITHOUT
assuming context from elsewhere in a source article (e.g. an article's title, or earlier sentences
establishing who "the subject" refers to).

You will be given:
- A SENTENCE (the only context available -- treat it as if it were shown to you with no surrounding
  text, no title, and no prior knowledge of what article it came from).
- A REFERENCE triple: the fact to check.

Judge whether the sentence, taken completely in isolation, actually identifies the SUBJECT and supports
the stated relationship to the OBJECT:

- If the subject is never named or unambiguously identifiable within this sentence alone (e.g. the
  sentence describes something about an implicit topic established elsewhere, uses only a pronoun with
  no antecedent inside the sentence, or does not reference the subject at all), the triple is NOT
  grounded.
- If the subject IS named in the sentence, but using a different form of the same name than the
  reference (e.g. "Charles Spurgeon Johnson" in the sentence vs. "Charles S. Johnson" in the reference,
  or "LaDonna Vita Tabbytite Harris" vs. "LaDonna Harris"), this still counts as grounded -- these are
  the same real-world entity, just written differently.
- If the object, or the relationship itself, is not actually stated or implied by the sentence, the
  triple is NOT grounded, even when the subject is clearly identified.
- Reasonable inference from what the sentence explicitly says is fine (e.g. a stated birth year implies
  a "date of birth" fact); inference that requires knowledge the sentence does not provide is not.

Return ONLY valid JSON (no explanation, no markdown), using one of:
{"grounded": true}
{"grounded": false, "missing": "subject"}
{"grounded": false, "missing": "object"}
{"grounded": false, "missing": "relation"}
{"grounded": false, "missing": "multiple"}
