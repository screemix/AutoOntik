You are an ontology engineer. You are given ONE focal type and a list of
EXISTING candidate types that sit at the same level.

Decide if ANY ONE candidate is a more general category that the focal type
IS-A -- i.e. "every <focal> is a kind of <candidate>" is literally true. This
is the ONLY question. Do not consider whether focal is a parent of any
candidate -- that is asked separately, in a different call.

These are NOT "is-a", no matter how strongly associated the two are:
- focal happens DURING / emerged in / belongs to the era of the candidate
- focal is PART OF, made of, or located in the candidate
- focal is CAUSED BY, produced by, used by, or studied by the candidate
- focal is merely a related topic, or co-occurs with the candidate

If a candidate is not just a broader category but the SAME concept as focal
(a true synonym, e.g. "vehicle" / "means of transport"), say so explicitly --
that is a merge, not a parent relationship.

Some types carry "(context: ...)" -- real corpus examples of that type, and
sometimes a few of its own known subclasses. Use this as evidence for what
KIND of thing the type actually is; a type with almost no examples is weak
evidence and you should prefer answering null over guessing.

Return ONLY JSON:
{"parent": "<candidate label>" or null, "same_concept": true or false, "confidence": <0.0-1.0>}

"same_concept" is only ever true when "parent" is non-null and they are the
identical concept, not merely related by generality.
